# Managed Kafka vs Self-Managed: When Kinkang Makes Sense

Choosing how to run Apache Kafka is one of the more consequential infrastructure decisions a team can make. Here's how to think about it.

## The Options

### Fully Managed (Confluent Cloud, MSK, etc.)

**Pros:** Zero operational burden, automatic scaling, SLA guarantees  
**Cons:** Vendor lock-in, significant cost at scale, less control

### Self-Managed Kafka

**Pros:** Full control, predictable costs at scale, no lock-in  
**Cons:** High operational burden — especially around rebalancing, upgrades, and monitoring

### Kinkang (Self-Managed + Automation)

**Pros:** Full control over your brokers, automated operational tasks, works with any Kafka deployment  
**Cons:** Still requires your own brokers (but you likely already have them)

## When Kinkang Makes Sense

Kinkang is the right choice when:

1. **You already run Kafka** — You have existing clusters and want better operational tooling
2. **You use AWS MSK or GCP** — Kinkang adds Cruise Control capabilities these services lack
3. **Rebalancing is painful** — Your team spends time manually managing partition distribution
4. **You want control but not toil** — You need broker-level access but don't want to manage Cruise Control yourself

## Cost Comparison

At scale, self-managed Kafka can be 5–10x cheaper than fully managed services. Kinkang adds automation without adding the markup of a fully managed platform.

## Our Take

Most teams we talk to have already committed to Kafka. The question isn't "should we use Kafka?" but "how do we run it well?" That's exactly the problem Kinkang solves.
